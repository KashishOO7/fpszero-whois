### How to start a career in Cyber Security with no experience in 2022.

*Photo by cottonbro from Pexels*

You are reading this because you have decided, you wanna step into this career. I will jump onto the roadmap that I follow. So, Let’s start with the basics. 
Do you understand the basics of computers and networking? How a computer works, how data travels, etc. 
If you wanna learn ethical hacking, first you need to learn **how something works and how can you break it or compromise it**. That’s the basic rule of this field. 
I’ll only be talking about the free resources that I use.

In general, Cyber Security prerequisites are:

1. Don’t get discouraged if you don’t have a technical degree. You need skills and experience, not a degree to showcase. Still, a degree is beneficial but not necessary.
2. A curious mind and a desire to learn new things daily.
3. Knowledge of languages such as C++, Java, Node, Python, Ruby, Go, or Power Shell is an added advantage. I’d suggest starting with python if you’re a beginner. You will grow as you learn.
4. Basics of Computers, Networking, Windows command shell, Linux OS.
5. Try to read research papers, Walkthroughs, the latest news, and hacks.

> I’m not a Security researcher yet. I’m still learning and exploring to this date. I’m just sharing my knowledge and experience here. You can choose any entry-level Certification to get, such as CCNA or CompTIA Security+ or eJPt. But of course, they are costly so I would not focus on them. I will only talk about the free-of-cost path for now.
>
> Even if you can’t afford the certs, just study for them. Make it your goal. You will learn something for sure.
>
> Imagine you are at the level of sec+ or CCNA. It will slightly be easier for you to get an entry-level job if you have that skills and knowledge. So, don’t tell yourself that if I can’t afford the exam, I will not study for it.

Where to practice and free resources:

1. CTFs: I’d put this on the top because it’s fun to solve CTFs (Capture the flag), you learn a lot from them and it’s free of cost. And if you get good at solving them, you can actually earn from them as well. They are just like Competitive programming but here you have to find the hidden flags. Some of the websites are: [picoCTF](https://picoctf.org/), [CTFtime](https://ctftime.org/) for ongoing and past CTF challenges, [Hacker 101 CTF](https://ctf.hacker101.com/). These are the beginner-friendly.
2. [Tryhackme](https://tryhackme.com/): This is surely one of the best platforms to learn cybersecurity. You can solve many free rooms here and it’s beginner-friendly. If you’re willing to spend a little amount then this can be worth the investment. The Tryhackme subscription starts at $9.05/monthly.
3. [Hackthebox](https://www.hackthebox.com/): HTB also has free labs to deploy and solve. It’s ideal for you if you wanna learn penetration testing. The subscription starts at around $11/monthly. I’d suggest first solving all the free HTB labs then start thinking about getting the subscription. To create an account on HTB, you need to hack their homepage and you can find many videos on YouTube for that. It’s cool. right?
4. Create your handwritten notes and store them for later use, it will help you learn and remember easily as it’s written by you in your handwriting. Download cheat sheets for Linux commands and all.
5. Here you also have Coursera, Pluralsight, YouTube as always because it’s free. You can get a 1-month Pluralsight subscription for free by signing up for [my visual studio benefits](https://my.visualstudio.com/Benefits).

> Note
>
> : If you’re stuck in any of the above, you can always check their walkthroughs. People always create write-ups about how they solved their CTF challenges or did a THM room or HTB lab. But first try to solve it by yourself.
>
> Give yourself at least 30 minutes to breakdown the challenge
>
> . If not, solutions and walkthroughs are always freely available for you.

> YouTubers that can help you
>
> : Professor Messer, John Hammond, NetworkChuck, Farah Hawa, David Bombal, Tech Raj, Pwn function, HackerSploit, Computerphile, InsiderPHD.

Take effective notes:
I personally use [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/). It’s really easy to use and there’s something special about it. Go and try it by yourself. Then there is OneNote by Microsoft and Notion. They all are free.
Another tip from me: Teach others whatever you have learned or studied.
As, sir Albert Einstein said, “_If you can’t explain it simply, you don’t understand it well enough_.” And I believe this to be true. Explaining others will help you realize how effectively you’re studying.
Join LinkedIn, Make connections and learn from other people’s journeys. We all maybe have the same destination but we follow different paths and we are unique in every way.

There are many different fields in cybersecurity like Digital Forensics, Bug Bounty, etc. I haven’t talked about them in this post because there’s so much to mention and the list is very long but I will surely talk about them later. 
If you have reached the end of this post thank you for reading. I wish you the best in your career. The more you practice, the better you will get at it.

Thanks for reading. Stay connected and **_remember_** **_Never stop learning_**.
