### The most stupid ways to get hacked.

> We all get hacked at some point in our life. Not like a massive attack but still.

*GIF via tenor*

Yes, the title says it all. Nothing is 100% immune to hacking. A system connected to the internet is safe just for the time being until someone finds a vulnerability in it.
It’s even easier to hack a human than a machine. And it’s called social engineering. Are you vulnerable to it? You think you’re not! But you are.
Anyone can get hacked even if they are a pro. You should know that everyone has a vulnerable side and vulnerable moments and they can be exploited by social engineering. 
To prevent being social engineered, always think twice before giving out info to anyone. And remember people you see are not always what they look like.

Ever heard of the term OSINT? This is the most legal way to hack you because OSINT is basically an act of piecing together all the information available publicly and it can reveal a lot about you without interacting with you directly unlike social engineering.
The things you share online like posting updates of wherever you go, leaving reviews online using your Gmail account, and using the same email for every purpose online can expose so much about you.
I recently realized that oversharing comes with a price. Think more than twice before posting something online because it’s the internet. Whatever you post here, it stays on the internet forever.

*GIF via tenor*

You are in public, you want to use the internet but then you realize you already used up all your data pack as of today or simply you don’t have your own internet connection. So, you search for any open WIFI connection and luckily you get a connection and it’s working. Now, you are checking your socials, messaging your partner, or doing some financial transactions. But wait, this sounds too good to be true. Oh crap! Maybe you weren’t that lucky. What happened right now is you were hacked and this is called an Evil twin attack. Now the attacker can steal your login credentials, spy on you, can even plant malware onto your device all with the help of a classic MITM attack. And this all becomes more fun and easy when you have a common password for all your social accounts, not using an updated OS, and don’t have antivirus software on your device. Oh C’mon, you’re in the 21st century and everything is connected to the internet. This is an online jungle we live in. You have to be your own savior.

Do you use mod APK or cracked software to bypass the premium version? If yes! Then don’t be surprised if every keystroke on your device is being sent to a 16-year-old hacker living in a basement somewhere on another continent. Hackers know you’re desperate for premium content and do not want to pay for the subscription and they leverage this by hiding malware or keyloggers in that software. And you already know that you are downloading it from a shady third-party website. So, you don’t always get the Gold.

Have you ever logged in to your accounts on a public computer anywhere? Who knows if there was some keylogger or if that computer was being controlled by someone using RDP? Control your urge to access your web accounts on any public computer even if it’s your school or college library. Usually, those systems are being monitored for safety purposes and all traffic goes through some proxy or a private network.

Don’t insert a random USB or disc you found somewhere, into your system. Because that little thing can harm us in so many ways that we can’t even think of. There’s a famous hacking show “Mr. Robot”, there was a scene in which a fake rapper was trying to sell his fake album in the form of a CD and he convinces a guy as the guy was already frustrated. So, that victim guy takes the CD to his home and inserts it into his PC. The disc had some sort of spyware in it and it steals all the data and gives a reverse shell to the hacker. And guess what happened next? The hacker blackmails the guy. Did you see how simple it was for that hacker to gain access to the system, it’s like the victim willingly gave him the access. Now, you know what not to do with a USB or disc that’s not yours.

Don’t sell your old device without re-writing the deleted files and folders. Read this here for more [info](https://medium.com/@kashishcharaya/are-files-from-your-computer-ever-permanently-deleted-or-where-do-they-go-466eeb6ba65f).
